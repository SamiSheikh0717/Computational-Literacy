ACTIVITY NAME: Rotational Energy Scan

GOALS

By completing this activity, students will:

- Analyze how molecular energy changes with rotational angle.
- Calculate relative energy from quantum-chemical energies.
- Convert energy differences from Hartrees to kcal/mol.
- Identify the lowest-energy conformation.
- Create a graph of molecular energy versus rotational angle.

EXPLANATION OF THE ACTIVITY

This activity examines how the energy of a molecule changes as a bond or molecular group is rotated.

The program contains a series of rotational angles and corresponding quantum-chemical energies. These energies represent calculations that could be obtained from a series of WebMO or Spartan calculations.

Because absolute quantum-chemical energies are large negative numbers, the program converts them into relative energies. The minimum energy is identified, and all other energies are expressed relative to that minimum.

The program then determines the rotational angle corresponding to the minimum energy and creates a graph showing the energy landscape.

EXPLANATION OF NEW CODE

The activity introduces NumPy arrays for storing multiple values:

angles = np.array([...])
energies = np.array([...])

This allows the entire set of rotational angles and energies to be manipulated computationally.

The minimum energy is found using:

minimum_energy = np.min(energies)

The program then calculates relative energy:

relative_energy = energies - minimum_energy

This subtracts the lowest energy from every energy in the array.

The relative energies are converted into kcal/mol:

relative_energy_kcal = relative_energy * 627.509

The program then finds the position of the minimum energy:

minimum_index = np.argmin(energies)

The corresponding angle is obtained using:

minimum_angle = angles[minimum_index]

The activity also introduces a for loop:

for i in range(len(angles)):

The loop allows the program to print every angle, energy, and relative energy without manually writing a separate print() statement for every data point.

Finally, Matplotlib is introduced:

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

The program uses:

plt.plot(angles, relative_energy_kcal, marker="o")

to visualize the relationship between rotational angle and relative energy.
